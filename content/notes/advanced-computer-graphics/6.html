Ray Tracing

6.1 Ray Casting

6.1.0.1 Light Rays

Three ideas about light rays:

  1. Light travels in straight lines (though this is wrong)
  2. Light rays do not collide with each other if they cross (though this is still wrong)
  3. Light rays travel from the light sources to the eye (the physics is invariant under path reversal)

6.1.0.2 Ray Casting Algorithm (Pseudocode)

for each pixel (x, y):
    ray = eye_ray(x, y)                    # from the eye through the pixel
    p = closest_intersection(ray, scene)
    if p exists:
        color = ambient
        for each light L:
            if not in_shadow(p, L):        # shadow ray from p toward L is unblocked
                color += shade(p, L)
        framebuffer[x][y] = color
    else:
        framebuffer[x][y] = background_color

6.2 Whitted-Style Ray Tracing

6.2.0.1 Recursive Ray Tracing (Pseudocode)

IntersectColor(o, d):
    p = closest_intersection(ray(o, d), scene)
    if p does not exist:
        return background_color
    color = ambient
    for each light L:
        color += shade(p, L)               # local shading term (shadow test as above)
    if surface at p is reflective:
        color += k_reflect * IntersectColor(p, reflect_dir)
    if surface at p is refractive:         # glass both reflects and refracts
        color += k_refract * IntersectColor(p, refract_dir)
    return color

6.3 Ray-Object Intersections

Definition 6.1 (Ray Equation) \[ \mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}, \quad 0 \le t < \infty. \]

  • \(\mathbf{r}(t)\): Point along the ray.
  • \(t\): “Time” along the ray; with a unit \(\mathbf{d}\), it is the distance from \(\mathbf{o}\).
  • \(\mathbf{o}\): Origin of the ray.
  • \(\mathbf{d}\): (Normalized) direction of the ray.

6.3.0.1 Ray Intersection with Sphere, Plane, and Triangle

6.3.0.2 Ray Intersection with Planar Polygon

  1. Intersect the ray with the polygon’s plane.
  2. Project the hit point and the polygon onto an axis-aligned plane by dropping the coordinate where the polygon’s normal has the largest absolute component. A fixed plane such as XY degenerates when the polygon is perpendicular to it.
  3. Shoot a ray from the projected hit point along one of the two remaining axes, and use the crossing test to decide inside/outside.

6.3.0.3 Möller-Trumbore Algorithm

Solve for \(t\) and the barycentric coordinates of the hit point at once, without first intersecting the triangle’s plane.

NoteFormulas

\[ \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d} = (1-b_1-b_2)\mathbf{P}_0 + b_1\mathbf{P}_1 + b_2\mathbf{P}_2, \]

\[ \begin{bmatrix} t \\ b_1 \\ b_2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{\mathbf{S}_1\cdot\mathbf{E}_1} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{S}_2\cdot\mathbf{E}_2 \\ \mathbf{S}_1\cdot\mathbf{S} \\ \mathbf{S}_2\cdot\mathbf{d} \end{bmatrix}. \]

  • \(\mathbf{P}_0,\mathbf{P}_1,\mathbf{P}_2\): Triangle vertices; \((1-b_1-b_2,\,b_1,\,b_2)\): Barycentric coordinates of the hit point.
  • \(\mathbf{E}_1=\mathbf{P}_1-\mathbf{P}_0\), \(\mathbf{E}_2=\mathbf{P}_2-\mathbf{P}_0\): Triangle edges; \(\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{o}-\mathbf{P}_0\).
  • \(\mathbf{S}_1=\mathbf{d}\times\mathbf{E}_2\), \(\mathbf{S}_2=\mathbf{S}\times\mathbf{E}_1\): Cross products reused across the three unknowns.

A valid hit needs \(t>0\) and \(b_1,\,b_2,\,1-b_1-b_2\ge 0\). If \(\mathbf{S}_1\cdot\mathbf{E}_1=0\), the ray is parallel to the triangle’s plane.

6.4 Accelerating Ray Tracing

Naive algorithm = #pixels ⨉ #triangles (⨉ #bounces).

6.4.0.1 Bounding Volumes

A quick way to avoid intersection tests is to bound a complex object with a simple volume: if the ray doesn’t hit the volume, it doesn’t hit the object.

6.4.0.2 AABB (Axis-Aligned Bounding Box) of a Transform

  • Tight: Transform the object itself (e.g., every mesh vertex), then take the min and max along each axis.
  • Conservative: Transform the 8 corners of the old AABB, then take their min and max. Cheaper, but the box can be larger than needed.

6.4.0.3 Ray Intersection with Axis-Aligned Box

  • For a 3D box, \(t_{\text{enter}} = \max\{t_{\min}\}\) and \(t_{\text{exit}} = \min\{t_{\max}\}\) over the three slabs.
  • The ray hits the box if \(t_{\text{enter}} < t_{\text{exit}}\) and \(t_{\text{exit}}>0\).
NoteWhy Axis-Aligned?

  • General plane through \(\mathbf{p}'\) with normal \(\mathbf{N}\): 3 subtractions, 6 multiplies, 1 division.

    \[ t = \frac{(\mathbf{p}'-\mathbf{o})\cdot\mathbf{N}}{\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{N}} \]

  • Slabs perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis: 1 subtraction, 1 division.

    \[ t = \frac{\mathbf{p}'_x-\mathbf{o}_x}{\mathbf{d}_x} \]

6.4.1 Uniform Spatial Partitions (Grids)

6.4.1.1 Build Acceleration Grid

  1. Find the bounding box.
  2. Create the grid.
  3. Store each object in the cells it overlaps.

6.4.1.2 Ray-Scene Intersection

  • Step through the grid in ray traversal order.
  • For each grid cell, test intersection with all objects stored in that cell.
  • Heuristic: #cells = C ⨉ #objs (C \(\approx\) 27 in 3D).
  • Uniform grids handle unevenly distributed objects poorly (the “teapot in a stadium” problem).

6.4.1.3 Triangle-Voxel Overlap

Search for a separating axis.

NoteSeparating Axis Test

Two convex polyhedra are disjoint if and only if they can be separated along a face normal of either one, or along the cross product of an edge from each. For a triangle and a box this gives 13 candidate axes; if none separates them, they overlap:

  1. Box normals \(\mathbf{e}_0,\mathbf{e}_1,\mathbf{e}_2\) (3 tests): Test the box against the triangle’s minimal AABB.
  2. Triangle normal \(\mathbf{n}\) (1 test): Plane/box test using only the two box diagonal vertices most aligned with \(\mathbf{n}\).
  3. \(\mathbf{a}_{ij}=\mathbf{e}_i\times\mathbf{f}_j\), \(i,j\in\{0,1,2\}\) (9 tests): Here \(\mathbf{f}_0=\mathbf{v}_1-\mathbf{v}_0\), \(\mathbf{f}_1=\mathbf{v}_2-\mathbf{v}_1\), \(\mathbf{f}_2=\mathbf{v}_0-\mathbf{v}_2\) are the triangle edges.

6.4.2 Spatial Partitioning

An object can be contained in multiple regions.

6.4.2.1 Oct-Tree

  • Top-down approach: Intersect root and find intersected children recursively.
  • Bottom-up approach: Find first leaf voxel intersected. Use neighbour finding to find next voxels.

6.4.2.2 BSP-Tree

  • General case uses arbitrary splitting planes.
  • Difficult to build, but traversal isn’t too bad.
  • Generally not used for rendering.

6.4.2.3 KD-Tree

  • Split position: Coordinate of the split plane along the axis.
  • No objects are stored in internal nodes.
  • Record \([t_{\text{start}},t_{\text{end}}]\) for each node to avoid traversing subtrees that the ray does not intersect.

6.4.3 Object Partitions

Bounding boxes for each set may overlap in space.

6.4.3.1 Bounding Volume Hierarchy (BVH)

  1. Find the bounding box.
  2. Recursively split the set of objects into two subsets.
  3. Stop when necessary.
  • Poor splitting can lead to unbalanced trees and poor performance.

6.5 Basic Radiometry

6.5.0.1 Radiant Energy

\(\displaystyle Q [\text{J = Joule}]\)

6.5.0.2 Radiant Flux (Power)

\(\displaystyle \Phi = \frac{dQ}{dt} [\text{W = Watt}] \text{ also }[ \text{lm} = \text{lumen} ]\)

6.5.0.3 Radiant Intensity

The radiant (luminous) intensity is the power per unit solid angle emitted by a point light source.

\(\displaystyle I(\omega) = \frac{d\Phi}{d\omega} [\text{W/sr}] \text{ also }[ \text{cd} = \text{candela} ]\)

6.5.0.4 Irradiance

The irradiance is the power per unit area incident on a surface point.

\(\displaystyle E(p) = \frac{d\Phi}{dA} [\text{W/m}^2] \text{ also }[ \text{lx} = \text{lux} ]\)

6.5.0.5 Radiance

  • The radiance (luminance) is the power emitted, reflected, transmitted or received by a surface, per unit solid angle, per projected unit area.

    \(\displaystyle L(p, \omega) = \frac{d^2\Phi}{d\omega dA \cos \theta} [\text{W/(sr m}^2)] \text{ also }[ \text{cd/m}^2 = \text{nit} ]\)

  • \(\theta\) is the angle between the surface normal and the direction \(\omega\).

  • Radiance is intensity per projected unit area, or irradiance per unit solid angle:

    \[ L(p, \omega) = \frac{dI(p, \omega)}{dA \cos \theta} = \frac{dE(p)}{d\omega \cos \theta}. \]

  • Hence \(dE(p) = L(p, \omega) \cos \theta \, d\omega\), and integrating over the unit hemisphere \(H^2\) gives

    \[ E(p) = \int_{H^2} L_i(p, \omega) \cos \theta \, d\omega. \]

6.6 Light Transport

6.6.0.1 Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF)

\(\displaystyle f_r(\omega_i \to \omega_r) = \frac{dL_r(\omega_r)}{dE_i(\omega_i)} = \frac{dL_r(\omega_r)}{L_i(\omega_i) \cos \theta_i \, d\omega_i} [\text{1/sr}]\)

6.6.0.2 The Rendering Equation

\[ \underbrace{L_r(x, \omega_r)}_{\substack{\text{Reflected Light} \\ \text{(Output Image)} \\ \text{Unknown}}} = \underbrace{L_e(x, \omega_r)}_{\substack{\text{Emission} \\ \text{Known}}} + \int_{\Omega} \underbrace{L_r(x', -\omega_i)}_{\substack{\text{Reflected Light} \\ \text{Unknown}}} \, \underbrace{f(x, \omega_i, \omega_r)}_{\substack{\text{BRDF} \\ \text{Known}}} \, \underbrace{\cos \theta_i}_{\substack{\text{Cosine of} \\ \text{Incident Angle} \\ \text{Known}}} \, d\omega_i \]
