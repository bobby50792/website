Shading

5.1 Shading

5.1.0.1 Shading Is Local

Compute light reflected toward camera at a specific shading point.

5.1.0.2 Diffuse Reflection

Light is scattered uniformly in all directions.

In general, light per unit area is proportional to

\[ \cos\theta = \mathbf{l}\cdot\mathbf{n}. \]

Definition 5.1 (Lambertian (Diffuse) Shading) \[ L_d = k_d\frac{I}{r^2}\max(0,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{l}). \]

  • \(L_d\): Diffusely reflected light at the shading point.
  • \(k_d\): Diffuse reflection coefficient (surface color).
  • \(I\): Point-light intensity.
  • \(r\): Distance from the shading point to the light source.
  • \(\mathbf{n}\): Unit surface normal at the shading point.
  • \(\mathbf{l}\): Unit direction from the shading point toward the light source.

Definition 5.2 (Specular Term (Blinn–Phong)) Intensity depends on view direction.

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{h} &= \frac{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{l}}{\|\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{l}\|}, \\ L_s &= k_s\frac{I}{r^2}\max(0,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{h})^p. \end{aligned} \]

  • \(\mathbf{v}\): Unit direction toward the camera; \(\mathbf{h}\): Halfway vector between \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{l}\).
  • \(L_s\): Specularly reflected light; \(k_s\): Specular reflection coefficient.
  • \(p\): Shininess exponent; larger values produce narrower highlights.

Definition 5.3 (Ambient Term) \[ L_a = k_a I_a. \]

  • \(L_a\): Reflected ambient light.
  • \(k_a\): Ambient reflection coefficient; \(I_a\): Ambient light intensity.

Definition 5.4 (Blinn–Phong Reflection Model) Ambient + diffuse + specular.

\[ \begin{aligned} L &= L_a + L_d + L_s \\ &= k_a I_a + k_d\frac{I}{r^2}\max(0,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{l}) \\ &\quad + k_s\frac{I}{r^2}\max(0,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{h})^p. \end{aligned} \]

5.1.0.3 Shading Frequencies

  • Flat shading (Face): One color per polygon. Not good for smooth surfaces.

  • Gouraud shading (Vertex): Interpolate vertex colors across polygon.

  • Phong Shading (Pixel): Interpolate normals across polygon and compute color per pixel. More expensive but better results.

Definition 5.5 (Per-Vertex Normals) Use the underlying geometry when available (e.g., radial normals for a sphere). Otherwise, average adjacent unit face normals \(\mathbf{N}_i\) and normalize:

\[ \mathbf{n}_v = \frac{\sum_i\mathbf{N}_i}{\left\|\sum_i\mathbf{N}_i\right\|}. \]

Definition 5.6 (Per-Pixel Normals) Barycentrically interpolate the triangle’s vertex normals, then normalize before shading:

\[ \begin{aligned} \tilde{\mathbf{n}} &= \alpha\mathbf{n}_1 + \beta\mathbf{n}_2 + \gamma\mathbf{n}_3, \\ \mathbf{n} &= \frac{\tilde{\mathbf{n}}}{\|\tilde{\mathbf{n}}\|}. \end{aligned} \]

Here \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are barycentric weights with \(\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1\).

5.2 Graphics Pipeline

5.2.0.1 Real-Time Rendering Pipeline

Convert 3D vertices into a pixel image.

  1. Vertex processing: Transform and project 3D vertices.
  2. Triangle processing: Assemble vertices into triangles and clip them to the view volume.
  3. Rasterization: Determine covered samples and generate fragments with interpolated attributes.
  4. Fragment processing: Compute fragment colors using shading and textures.
  5. Framebuffer operations: Apply depth/stencil tests and blending, then write the final image for display.

Data flow: Vertices → triangles → fragments → shaded fragments → pixels.

5.3 Texturing

5.3.1 Texture Mapping

5.3.1.1 UV Unwrapping by Optimization

Assign 2D \((u,v)\) coordinates to mesh vertices to flatten the surface with minimal distortion.

  • Preserve surface distances and/or triangle angles as closely as possible.
  • Cuts (seams) are usually required to unfold the surface into UV charts.

5.3.2 Barycentric Coordinates

Definition 5.7 (Barycentric Coordinates) For a point \(P\) inside a nondegenerate triangle \(ABC\), write \(P=\alpha A+\beta B+\gamma C\).

Let \(A_A,A_B,A_C\) be the areas of the opposite subtriangles \(PBC,PCA,PAB\), respectively.

\[ \begin{aligned} \alpha &= \frac{A_A}{A_A+A_B+A_C}, \\ \beta &= \frac{A_B}{A_A+A_B+A_C}, \\ \gamma &= \frac{A_C}{A_A+A_B+A_C}. \end{aligned} \]

The weights are nonnegative and satisfy \(\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1\).

Definition 5.8 (Attribute Interpolation) Linearly interpolate vertex values using the same weights:

\[ V = \alpha V_A + \beta V_B + \gamma V_C. \]

The values can be positions, UV coordinates, colors, normals, depth, or material attributes.

NoteWhy Perspective Needs Correction

Barycentric coordinates are generally not preserved under perspective projection; attributes such as UV coordinates require perspective-correct interpolation.

5.3.3 Texture Queries

5.3.3.1 Texture Magnification - Easy Case

Bilinear, Bicubic interpolation of texels.

5.3.3.2 Texture Minification - Hard Case

  • Screen Pixel Footprint in Texture

Definition 5.9 (Mipmap Level Selection) Each level halves the texture width and height. Estimate the footprint size from neighboring screen samples:

\[ \begin{aligned} L_x &= \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2 +\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right)^2}, \\ L_y &= \sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)^2 +\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right)^2}, \\ L &= \max(L_x,L_y), \\ D &= \log_2 L. \end{aligned} \]

  • Use base-level texels for \((u,v)\) and screen pixels for \((x,y)\); scale normalized UVs by texture width and height first.

  • Clamp \(D\) to the available levels, and blend adjacent levels when \(D\) is fractional (trilinear filtering).

  • Limitation: Overblur

5.3.3.3 Anisotropic Filtering

  • Ripmaps and summed-area tables: Support filtering over axis-aligned rectangular regions.
  • Limitation: Diagonal footprints are poorly matched and may cause overblurring.

EWA filtering (Elliptical Weighted Average):

  • Use multiple texture lookups and compute a weighted average.
  • A mipmap hierarchy helps reduce sampling cost.
  • Approximate tilted or irregular footprints with elliptical filters.

5.3.4 Applications of Textures

In modern GPUs, texture = memory + range query (filtering)

5.3.4.1 Environment Mapping

  • Use a texture to represent the surrounding environment for reflections.

  • Prone to distortion (top and bottom parts)

5.3.4.2 Cube Map

  • A vector maps to cube point along that direction. The cube is textured with 6 square textures, one for each face.

  • Much less distortion

5.3.4.3 Bump Mapping

  • Perturb surface normal per pixel (for shading computations only)

  • “Height shift” per texel defined by a texture

Proposition 5.1 (Normal Perturbation (Local Coordinates))  

  • In 2D, estimate the slope \(s \approx c[h(p+1)-h(p)]\). The tangent is \((1,s)\), so the unit normal is \(\mathbf{n}=\operatorname{normalize}(-s,1)\).

  • In 3D, estimate slopes in both texture directions:

    \[ \begin{aligned} d_u &\approx c_1[h(u+\Delta u,v)-h(u,v)], \\ d_v &\approx c_2[h(u,v+\Delta v)-h(u,v)], \\ \mathbf{n}_{\text{local}} &= \operatorname{normalize}(-d_u,-d_v,1). \end{aligned} \]

NoteWhy the Normal Has This Form

The normal is perpendicular to both tangents \((1,0,d_u)\) and \((0,1,d_v)\). Steps \(\Delta u,\Delta v\) span one texel; the coefficients include spacing conversion and bump strength. Transform the local normal into the lighting coordinate system before shading.

Actually moves the vertices

5.4 Shadow Mapping

5.4.0.1 Shadow Mapping Algorithm

A camera-visible point is unshadowed if it is also visible from the light.

NoteLimitations of Shadow Mapping
  • Hard shadows (point lights only).
  • Quality depends on shadow-map resolution; finite sampling can cause aliasing.
  • Involves equality comparison of floating point depth values, which means issues of scale, bias, and tolerance.
  • Rasterization cannot handle global effects well.

5.5 Supplementary Notes

5.5.1 Key Results

  • Definition 5.1: Diffuse light depends on \(\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{l}\) and is independent of the view direction.
  • Definition 5.4: \(L=L_a+L_d+L_s\), the sum of ambient, diffuse, and specular terms.
  • Definition 5.7: The weights of a point in a triangle are the area ratios of its opposite subtriangles.
  • Definition 5.8: Vertex attributes are interpolated with the barycentric weights, with a perspective correction after projection.
  • Definition 5.9: \(D=\log_2 L\) selects a mipmap level from the footprint size \(L\) of a screen pixel in the texture.
