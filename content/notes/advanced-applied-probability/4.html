Martingales

Filtrations, stopping times, optional stopping, martingale convergence, and uniform integrability.

4.1 Conditional Expectation

Work on a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal F_0,\mathbb P)\) with sub-\(\sigma\)-fields \(\mathcal F,\mathcal F_1,\mathcal F_2\subseteq\mathcal F_0\).

Theorem 4.1 (Basic Properties of Conditional Expectation) Let \(X,Y\) be integrable random variables, with conditional expectation \(\omega\mapsto\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F](\omega)\). The following hold almost surely:

  1. Linearity. For any \(a\in\mathbb R\),

    \[ \mathbb E[aX+Y\mid\mathcal F] =a\,\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]+\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]. \]

  2. Monotonicity. If \(X\le Y\) a.s., then \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]\le\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]\) a.s.

  3. Monotone convergence. If \(X_n\ge0\) and \(X_n\uparrow X\) a.s. with \(\mathbb E X<\infty\), then \(\mathbb E[X_n\mid\mathcal F]\uparrow\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]\) a.s.

NoteProof Sketch

Existence of \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]\) follows by applying the Radon–Nikodym theorem to \(A\mapsto\int_A X^\pm\,d\mathbb P\) on \((\Omega,\mathcal F)\). For (i), \(a\,\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]+\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]\) is \(\mathcal F\)-measurable and has integral \(\int_A(aX+Y)\,d\mathbb P\) on every \(A\in\mathcal F\), so uniqueness a.s. gives equality. For (ii), let \(A=\{\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]>\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]\}\in\mathcal F\); then \(\int_A(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]-\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F])\,d\mathbb P=\int_A(X-Y)\,d\mathbb P\le0\), forcing \(\mathbb P(A)=0\). For (iii), monotonicity gives \(\mathbb E[X_n\mid\mathcal F]\uparrow Z\) a.s. for an \(\mathcal F\)-measurable \(Z\ge0\), and monotone convergence yields \(\int_A Z\,d\mathbb P=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_A X_n\,d\mathbb P=\int_A X\,d\mathbb P\) for every \(A\in\mathcal F\).

Theorem 4.2 (Conditioning Known Random Variables) If \(X\) is \(\mathcal F\)-measurable (\(X\in\mathcal F\)) and \(\mathbb E|X|<\infty\), then

\[ \mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]=X \qquad\text{a.s.} \]

More generally, if \(X\in\mathcal F\) and \(\mathbb E|Y|,\mathbb E|XY|<\infty\), then \(\mathbb E[XY\mid\mathcal F]=X\,\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]\) a.s.

NoteProof Sketch

The random variable \(X\) itself satisfies both conditions of conditional expectation: \(X\) is \(\mathcal F\)-measurable and \(\int_A X\,d\mathbb P=\int_A X\,d\mathbb P\) for every \(A\in\mathcal F\), so \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F](\omega)=X(\omega)\) for a.e. \(\omega\). For the pull-out rule, \(X=\mathbf 1_B\) (\(B\in\mathcal F\)) follows from \(\int_A\mathbf 1_B Y\,d\mathbb P=\int_{A\cap B}\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]\,d\mathbb P=\int_A\mathbf 1_B\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal F]\,d\mathbb P\), and the general case follows by linearity, Theorem 4.1(iii), and splitting into positive and negative parts.

Theorem 4.3 (Conditioning Under Independence) If \(X\) is independent of \(\mathcal F\) (i.e., \(\sigma(X)\perp\!\!\!\perp\mathcal F\)) and \(\mathbb E|X|<\infty\), then

\[ \mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]=\mathbb E[X] \qquad\text{a.s.} \]

NoteProof Sketch

The constant random variable \(c(\omega)=\mathbb E[X]\) is \(\mathcal F\)-measurable. For every \(A\in\mathcal F\), \(\sigma(X)\perp\!\!\!\perp\mathcal F\) implies that \(X\) and \(\mathbf 1_A\) are independent (when \(\mathbb P(A)>0\), \(\mathbb E[X\mid A]=\mathbb E[X]\)), so by product expectation,

\[ \int_A X\,d\mathbb P =\mathbb E[X\mathbf 1_A] =\mathbb E[X]\,\mathbb P(A) =\int_A\mathbb E[X]\,d\mathbb P. \]

By uniqueness in Kolmogorov’s definition, \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]=\mathbb E[X]\) a.s.

Theorem 4.4 (Tower Property and Conditional Jensen’s Inequality) Let \(X\) be an integrable random variable.

  1. Tower property. If \(\mathcal F_1\subseteq\mathcal F_2\), then

    \[ \mathbb E\bigl[\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_2]\bigm|\mathcal F_1\bigr] =\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_1] =\mathbb E\bigl[\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_1]\bigm|\mathcal F_2\bigr] \qquad\text{a.s.} \]

    In particular, \(\mathbb E\bigl[\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]\bigr]=\mathbb E[X]\).

  2. Conditional Jensen’s inequality. If \(\varphi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) is convex and \(\mathbb E|\varphi(X)|<\infty\), then

    \[ \varphi\bigl(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]\bigr) \le\mathbb E[\varphi(X)\mid\mathcal F] \qquad\text{a.s.} \]

    In particular, \(\|\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F]\|_p\le\|X\|_p\) for all \(1\le p<\infty\).

NoteProof Sketch

In (i), the first equality holds because every \(A\in\mathcal F_1\) also lies in \(\mathcal F_2\), so \(\int_A\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_2]\,d\mathbb P=\int_A X\,d\mathbb P\); the second holds by Theorem 4.2 since \(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_1]\in\mathcal F_2\).

In (ii), write \(\varphi(x)=\sup_{q\in\mathbb Q}L_q(x)\) as the supremum of supporting lines \(L_q(x)=\varphi(q)+\varphi'_+(q)(x-q)\) at rational points. By Theorem 4.1, \(\mathbb E[\varphi(X)\mid\mathcal F]\ge\mathbb E[L_q(X)\mid\mathcal F]=L_q(\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F])\) a.s. for each \(q\in\mathbb Q\), and taking the countable supremum gives the bound.

4.2 Filtrations and Martingales

Definition 4.1 (Filtration and Adapted Process) A filtration on \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\) is an increasing sequence of sub-\(\sigma\)-fields

\[ \mathcal F_0\subseteq\mathcal F_1\subseteq\mathcal F_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal F, \]

with \(\mathcal F_\infty=\sigma\bigl(\bigcup_{n=0}^\infty\mathcal F_n\bigr)\). A sequence \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge0}\) is adapted to \(\{\mathcal F_n\}\) if \(X_n\in\mathcal F_n\) for every \(n\ge0\), and \(\{H_n\}_{n\ge1}\) is predictable if \(H_n\in\mathcal F_{n-1}\) for every \(n\ge1\).

Definition 4.2 (Martingale, Submartingale, and Supermartingale) A process \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge0}\) is a martingale with respect to \(\{\mathcal F_n\}_{n\ge0}\) if:

  1. \(X_n\) is adapted to \(\mathcal F_n\) for every \(n\ge0\).

  2. \(\mathbb E|X_n|<\infty\) for every \(n\ge0\).

  3. \(\mathbb E[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]=X_n\) a.s. for every \(n\ge0\).

Replacing (iii) by \(\mathbb E[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]\ge X_n\) a.s. defines a submartingale (so \(\mathbb E X_n\) is nondecreasing), and replacing it by \(\mathbb E[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]\le X_n\) a.s. defines a supermartingale (so \(\mathbb E X_n\) is nonincreasing).

NoteExample: Canonical Martingales
  • Random walk: If \(\xi_1,\xi_2,\ldots\) are independent with \(\mathbb E\xi_k=0\), \(\mathcal F_n=\sigma(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\), and \(S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n\xi_k\) (\(S_0=0\)), then \(\{S_n\}\) is a martingale by Theorem 4.2 and Theorem 4.3; if \(\operatorname{Var}(\xi_k)=\sigma_k^2<\infty\), then \(S_n^2-\sum_{k=1}^n\sigma_k^2\) is also a martingale.
  • Product martingale: If \(\xi_1,\xi_2,\ldots\ge0\) are independent with \(\mathbb E\xi_k=1\), then \(M_n=\prod_{k=1}^n\xi_k\) (\(M_0=1\)) is a nonnegative martingale.
  • Doob’s martingale: For any \(X\in L^1\) and filtration \(\{\mathcal F_n\}\), \(X_n=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_n]\) is a martingale by Theorem 4.4(i).

4.3 Martingale Transforms and Stopping Times

Definition 4.3 (Martingale Transform) For adapted \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge0}\) and predictable \(\{H_n\}_{n\ge1}\), the martingale transform is \((H\cdot X)_0=0\) and

\[ (H\cdot X)_n=\sum_{m=1}^n H_m(X_m-X_{m-1}), \qquad n\ge1. \]

Theorem 4.5 (Preservation Under Martingale Transforms) Let \(\{H_n\}_{n\ge1}\) be predictable with \(|H_n|\le C_n<\infty\) for each \(n\).

  1. If \(\{X_n\}\) is a submartingale (resp. supermartingale) and \(H_n\ge0\), then \(\{(H\cdot X)_n\}\) is a submartingale (resp. supermartingale).

  2. If \(\{X_n\}\) is a martingale, then \(\{(H\cdot X)_n\}\) is a martingale.

NoteProof Sketch

Boundedness of \(H_m\) ensures \(\mathbb E|(H\cdot X)_n|<\infty\). Since \((H\cdot X)_n\in\mathcal F_n\) and \(H_{n+1}\in\mathcal F_n\), Theorem 4.1(i) and Theorem 4.2 give \(\mathbb E[(H\cdot X)_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]=(H\cdot X)_n+H_{n+1}\,\mathbb E[X_{n+1}-X_n\mid\mathcal F_n]\) a.s., which yields the required inequality or equality.

Definition 4.4 (Stopping Time and Stopped σ-Field) A random variable \(T:\Omega\to\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}\) is a stopping time if

\[ \{T=n\}\in\mathcal F_n \qquad\text{for every }n\ge0 \]

(equivalently, \(\{T\le n\}\in\mathcal F_n\) for every \(n\ge0\)). For instance, the first hitting time \(T_B=\inf\{n\ge0:X_n\in B\}\) of an adapted process is a stopping time since \(\{T_B\le n\}=\bigcup_{k=0}^n\{X_k\in B\}\in\mathcal F_n\).

The stopped \(\sigma\)-field is \(\mathcal F_T=\{A\in\mathcal F_\infty:A\cap\{T\le n\}\in\mathcal F_n\text{ for all }n\ge0\}\).

4.4 Martingale Convergence

Lemma 4.1 (Doob’s Upcrossing Inequality) Let \(\{X_m\}_{m\ge0}\) be a submartingale and fix \(a<b\). Define stopping times \(N_0=-1\) and, for \(k\ge1\),

\[ N_{2k-1}=\inf\{m>N_{2k-2}:X_m\le a\}, \qquad N_{2k}=\inf\{m>N_{2k-1}:X_m\ge b\}, \]

and let \(U_n[a,b]=\sup\{k\ge1:N_{2k}\le n\}\) be the number of upcrossings of \([a,b]\) by time \(n\). Then

\[ (b-a)\,\mathbb E\,U_n[a,b] \le\mathbb E(X_n-a)^+-\mathbb E(X_0-a)^+ \le\mathbb E X_n^++|a|. \]

NoteProof Sketch

Set \(Y_m=a+(X_m-a)^+\), which is a submartingale (by Theorem 4.4(ii)) with the same upcrossings of \([a,b]\) as \(\{X_m\}\). Let \(H_m=\sum_{k\ge1}\mathbf 1_{\{N_{2k-1}<m\le N_{2k}\}}\in\mathcal F_{m-1}\). Each completed upcrossing gains at least \(b-a\), and any incomplete final upcrossing starts at \(Y_{N_{2r-1}}=a\le Y_n\), so \((b-a)U_n[a,b]\le(H\cdot Y)_n\). Since \(1-H_m\ge0\) is predictable, \(\mathbb E[((1-H)\cdot Y)_n]\ge0\) by Theorem 4.5, giving \((b-a)\mathbb E\,U_n[a,b]\le\mathbb E(H\cdot Y)_n\le\mathbb E(Y_n-Y_0)\).

Theorem 4.6 (Martingale Convergence Theorem) If \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge0}\) is a submartingale with \(\sup_{n\ge0}\mathbb E X_n^+<\infty\), then as \(n\to\infty\), \(X_n\) converges almost surely to a random variable \(X_\infty\) with \(\mathbb E|X_\infty|<\infty\).

In particular, every nonnegative supermartingale (\(X_n\ge0\)) converges almost surely to \(X_\infty\) with \(\mathbb E X_\infty\le\mathbb E X_0\).

NoteProof Sketch

For all rationals \(a<b\), monotone convergence and Lemma 4.1 give \(\mathbb E[\lim_{n\to\infty}U_n[a,b]]\le(\sup_n\mathbb E X_n^++|a|)/(b-a)<\infty\), so the total upcrossings of every rational interval \([a,b]\) are a.s. finite, forcing \(\liminf_n X_n=\limsup_n X_n=:X_\infty\) a.s. Using \(|X_n|=2X_n^+-X_n\) and \(\mathbb E X_n\ge\mathbb E X_0\) gives \(\sup_n\mathbb E|X_n|\le 2\sup_n\mathbb E X_n^+-\mathbb E X_0<\infty\), and Fatou’s lemma yields \(\mathbb E|X_\infty|<\infty\). For a nonnegative supermartingale, apply this to \(-X_n\le0\) and Fatou’s lemma.

Theorem 4.7 (Doob’s Maximal Inequalities)  

  1. Maximal inequality. If \(\{X_m\}_{m\ge0}\) is a submartingale and \(\lambda>0\), then for \(A=\bigl\{\max_{0\le m\le n}X_m\ge\lambda\bigr\}\),

    \[ \lambda\,\mathbb P\left(\max_{0\le m\le n}X_m\ge\lambda\right) \le\mathbb E[X_n\mathbf 1_A] \le\mathbb E[X_n^+\mathbf 1_A] \le\mathbb E X_n^+. \]

  2. \(L^p\) maximal inequality. If \(\{X_m\}_{m\ge0}\) is a martingale or a nonnegative submartingale and \(1<p<\infty\), then for \(\bar X_n=\max_{0\le m\le n}|X_m|\),

    \[ \|\bar X_n\|_p\le\frac{p}{p-1}\,\|X_n\|_p. \]

NoteProof Sketch

For (i), partition \(A\) by the first hitting time into disjoint sets \(A_m=\{X_0<\lambda,\ldots,X_{m-1}<\lambda,\,X_m\ge\lambda\}\in\mathcal F_m\) (\(0\le m\le n\)). Since \(X_m\le\mathbb E[X_n\mid\mathcal F_m]\) a.s.,

\[ \lambda\,\mathbb P(A) =\sum_{m=0}^n\lambda\,\mathbb P(A_m) \le\sum_{m=0}^n\int_{A_m}X_m\,d\mathbb P \le\sum_{m=0}^n\int_{A_m}X_n\,d\mathbb P =\mathbb E[X_n\mathbf 1_A]. \]

For (ii), assume \(\|X_n\|_p<\infty\) and truncate \(Y=\bar X_n\wedge M\). Applying Fubini’s theorem, part (i) for \(\{|X_m|\}\), and Hölder’s inequality gives \(\mathbb E Y^p=\int_0^M p\lambda^{p-1}\mathbb P(\bar X_n\ge\lambda)\,d\lambda\le\frac{p}{p-1}\mathbb E[|X_n|Y^{p-1}]\le\frac{p}{p-1}\|X_n\|_p\|Y\|_p^{p-1}\). Dividing by \(\|Y\|_p^{p-1}\) and letting \(M\uparrow\infty\) yields the bound.

Theorem 4.8 (Lᵖ Martingale Convergence Theorem) Let \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge0}\) be a martingale and \(1<p<\infty\). Then \(\sup_{n\ge0}\mathbb E|X_n|^p<\infty\) if and only if \(X_n\) converges almost surely and in \(L^p\) to a random variable \(X_\infty\in L^p\) (in which case \(X_n=\mathbb E[X_\infty\mid\mathcal F_n]\) a.s.).

In particular, for \(p=2\), martingale increments are orthogonal, \(\mathbb E X_n^2=\mathbb E X_0^2+\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb E[(X_k-X_{k-1})^2]\), so \(X_n\) converges a.s. and in \(L^2\) if and only if \(\sum_{k=1}^\infty\mathbb E[(X_k-X_{k-1})^2]<\infty\).

NoteProof Sketch

If \(\sup_n\|X_n\|_p<\infty\), Theorem 4.6 gives \(X_n\to X_\infty\) a.s., while Theorem 4.7(ii) and monotone convergence give \(\sup_n|X_n|\in L^p\); dominated convergence then yields \(\mathbb E|X_n-X_\infty|^p\to0\), and passing \(n\to\infty\) in \(\int_A X_m\,d\mathbb P=\int_A X_n\,d\mathbb P\) (\(A\in\mathcal F_m\)) gives \(X_m=\mathbb E[X_\infty\mid\mathcal F_m]\) a.s. The converse is immediate. For \(p=2\), \(\mathbb E[(X_n-X_m)(X_k-X_j)]=0\) (\(j<k\le m<n\)) follows by conditioning on \(\mathcal F_m\).

4.5 Uniform Integrability

Definition 4.5 (Uniform Integrability) A family of random variables \(\{X_i\}_{i\in I}\) is uniformly integrable if

\[ \lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_{i\in I} \mathbb E\bigl[|X_i|\,\mathbf 1_{\{|X_i|>M\}}\bigr]=0. \]

NoteWhy L¹-Boundedness Is Not Enough at p = 1

For \(p=1\), an \(L^1\)-bounded martingale need not converge in \(L^1\): if \(M_n=\prod_{k=1}^n\xi_k\) with i.i.d. \(\mathbb P(\xi_k=2)=\mathbb P(\xi_k=0)=\tfrac12\), then \(\mathbb E M_n=1\) for every \(n\), yet \(M_n\to0\) a.s. Uniform integrability is the exact condition that prevents mass from escaping to infinity.

Theorem 4.9 (Sufficient Conditions for Uniform Integrability)  

  1. Domination and \(L^p\) bounds. \(\{X_i\}_{i\in I}\) is uniformly integrable if \(|X_i|\le Y\) a.s. for some \(Y\in L^1\), or if \(\sup_{i\in I}\mathbb E|X_i|^p<\infty\) for some \(p>1\).

  2. Conditional expectations of a single \(L^1\) variable. If \(\mathbb E|X|<\infty\), then \(\bigl\{\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]:\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F_0\text{ is a sub-}\sigma\text{-field}\bigr\}\) is uniformly integrable.

NoteProof Sketch

In (i), \(|X_i|\le Y\in L^1\) gives \(\mathbb E[|X_i|\mathbf 1_{\{|X_i|>M\}}]\le\mathbb E[Y\mathbf 1_{\{Y>M\}}]\to0\) by dominated convergence, while for \(p>1\), \(|X_i|\mathbf 1_{\{|X_i|>M\}}\le M^{-(p-1)}|X_i|^p\).

In (ii), let \(Y_{\mathcal G}=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G]\) and \(A_{\mathcal G}=\{|Y_{\mathcal G}|>M\}\in\mathcal G\). By Theorem 4.4(ii) and Markov’s inequality, \(|Y_{\mathcal G}|\le\mathbb E[|X|\mid\mathcal G]\) a.s. and \(\mathbb P(A_{\mathcal G})\le\mathbb E|X|/M\to0\) uniformly in \(\mathcal G\) as \(M\to\infty\). Hence \(\mathbb E[|Y_{\mathcal G}|\mathbf 1_{A_{\mathcal G}}]\le\int_{A_{\mathcal G}}|X|\,d\mathbb P\to0\) uniformly in \(\mathcal G\) because \(X\in L^1\).

Theorem 4.10 (Vitali Convergence Theorem) Let \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge1}\) be integrable random variables. Then \(X_n\to X\) in \(L^1\) (with \(X\in L^1\)) if and only if \(X_n\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}X\) and \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge1}\) is uniformly integrable.

NoteProof Sketch

(\(\Rightarrow\)) Markov’s inequality gives \(X_n\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}X\), and approximating \(X_n\) by \(X\in L^1\) for \(n>N\) leaves finitely many \(L^1\) variables \(\{X_1,\ldots,X_N,X\}\), giving uniform integrability.

(\(\Leftarrow\)) Passing to an a.s. convergent subsequence gives \(X\in L^1\) by Fatou’s lemma. Truncating via \(\psi_M(x)=(x\wedge M)\vee(-M)\),

\[ \mathbb E|X_n-X| \le\mathbb E\bigl[|X_n|\mathbf 1_{\{|X_n|>M\}}\bigr] +\mathbb E|\psi_M(X_n)-\psi_M(X)| +\mathbb E\bigl[|X|\mathbf 1_{\{|X|>M\}}\bigr]. \]

Fix \(M\) large so the outer terms are small by uniform integrability and \(X\in L^1\), then send \(n\to\infty\) in the middle term using the bounded convergence theorem.

Theorem 4.11 (Uniformly Integrable Martingales and Lévy’s Upward Theorem) For a martingale \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge0}\), the following are equivalent:

  1. \(\{X_n\}_{n\ge0}\) is uniformly integrable.

  2. \(X_n\) converges almost surely and in \(L^1\) to some \(X_\infty\in L^1\).

  3. \(X_n\) converges in \(L^1\) to some \(X_\infty\in L^1\).

  4. There exists \(X\in L^1\) such that \(X_n=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_n]\) a.s. for every \(n\ge0\).

Moreover, when these hold:

  • Lévy’s upward theorem: If \(X_n=\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_n]\) for \(X\in L^1\), then \(X_n\to\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal F_\infty]\) a.s. and in \(L^1\), and in general \(X_n=\mathbb E[X_\infty\mid\mathcal F_n]\) a.s.
  • Optional stopping: For any stopping times \(S\le T\le\infty\) (with \(X_T=X_\infty\) on \(\{T=\infty\}\)), \(\mathbb E[X_T\mid\mathcal F_S]=X_S\) a.s. and \(\mathbb E X_T=\mathbb E X_0\).
NoteProof Sketch

(i) \(\Rightarrow\) (ii): Uniform integrability implies \(\sup_n\mathbb E|X_n|<\infty\), so Theorem 4.6 gives \(X_n\to X_\infty\) a.s. and Theorem 4.10 upgrades this to \(L^1\) convergence. (ii) \(\Rightarrow\) (iii) is immediate; (iii) \(\Rightarrow\) (iv) follows by letting \(n\to\infty\) in \(\int_A X_m\,d\mathbb P=\int_A X_n\,d\mathbb P\) (\(A\in\mathcal F_m\)), giving \(X_m=\mathbb E[X_\infty\mid\mathcal F_m]\) a.s.; and (iv) \(\Rightarrow\) (i) is Theorem 4.9(ii).

For Lévy’s upward theorem, \(\int_A X_\infty\,d\mathbb P=\int_A X\,d\mathbb P\) holds on \(\bigcup_{m=0}^\infty\mathcal F_m\) and extends to \(\mathcal F_\infty\) by the \(\pi\)-\(\lambda\) theorem. For optional stopping, summing \(\int_{A\cap\{T=n\}}X_T\,d\mathbb P=\int_{A\cap\{T=n\}}X_\infty\,d\mathbb P\) over \(n\ge0\) (for \(A\in\mathcal F_T\)) gives \(X_T=\mathbb E[X_\infty\mid\mathcal F_T]\) a.s., and Theorem 4.4(i) yields \(\mathbb E[X_T\mid\mathcal F_S]=X_S\) a.s. since \(\mathcal F_S\subseteq\mathcal F_T\).
